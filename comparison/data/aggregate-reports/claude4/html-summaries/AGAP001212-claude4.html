Summary

This gene exhibits highly tissue-specific expression in Anopheles gambiae with strongest enrichment in
  • digestive tissues (midgut, salivary glands, gastric caeca)
  • immune-related hemocytes and hemolymph
  • male reproductive organs, particularly testes
. Expression shows clear developmental progression from low levels in embryonic/larval stages to peak expression in adults, with consistent male-biased and female-biased patterns depending on tissue context. The gene displays circadian regulation in head tissues and responds to various physiological challenges including blood feeding, pathogen infection, and insecticide exposure. Given its enrichment in digestive and immune tissues combined with pathogen responsiveness, this gene likely functions in innate immunity or digestive physiology, potentially serving dual roles in nutrient processing and antimicrobial defense.
