Summary

This gene exhibits extreme adult-specific expression in Anopheles gambiae, with 256-fold upregulation from larvae to adults and expression levels reaching the 99.9th percentile in adult tissues. Tissue distribution shows remarkable specialization: salivary glands display the highest expression (16,756 TPM, 99.99th percentile), followed by strong anterior gut regionalization (200-fold enrichment in proventriculus) and testes-specific expression (1,200-fold over accessory glands). Importantly, the gene responds dramatically to Plasmodium infection, with 10-1,000-fold upregulation in infected midguts, particularly in mated females. The expression pattern suggests potential roles in digestion, vector-parasite interactions, and male reproduction, with the salivary gland enrichment indicating possible involvement in blood-feeding or pathogen transmission.
