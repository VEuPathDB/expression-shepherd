Summary

This gene exhibits extraordinary blood meal responsiveness in Anopheles gambiae, with expression increasing 100-1000 fold within 24 hours post-feeding across multiple tissues including fat body, midgut, and antennae. The gene shows pronounced sexual dimorphism, with consistently high expression in females (>99th percentile) and uniformly low expression in males across all tissues. Expression is also mating-responsive, increasing significantly in mated versus virgin females. Additionally, the gene demonstrates developmental stage-specific upregulation in gravid females compared to larvae and virgin females. This dramatic blood meal and reproductive-associated expression pattern strongly suggests involvement in egg development and reproductive physiology, likely encoding a protein critical for vitellogenesis or other post-blood meal metabolic processes essential for mosquito reproduction.
