Summary

This gene exhibits one of the most dramatic sex-biased expression patterns in Anopheles gambiae, with female expression levels exceeding males by over 100-fold across multiple experiments. The gene shows particularly striking tissue specificity, with salivary glands displaying 300-700 fold higher expression than midgut tissues. Expression remains stable across the adult female lifespan but is dramatically suppressed following blood feeding, suggesting critical roles in female-specific physiology that are temporarily downregulated during reproductive cycles. The extreme female bias combined with salivary gland enrichment strongly suggests this gene functions in female-specific processes related to blood feeding, pathogen transmission, or reproductive biology, making it a candidate for understanding sex-specific vector competence mechanisms.
