Developmentally regulated gene with somatic tissue enrichment, chemosensory expression, and reproductive and infection responses

Summary

This gene exhibits complex spatiotemporal regulation across multiple biological contexts in Anopheles gambiae.
  • Expression increases dramatically during late embryogenesis (16-fold from early to late stages) and declines progressively during adult aging (2.5-3-fold decrease over 30 days).
  • In adults, the gene shows strong tissue-specific patterns with highest expression in somatic tissues (carcass), salivary glands, anterior gut regions (proventriculus), and maxillary palps (male-biased), while remaining low in reproductive organs.
  • Expression responds to physiological states: downregulated following blood-feeding, upregulated in the spermatheca after mating and in response to 20-hydroxyecdysone, and substantially induced (8-10-fold) during malaria parasite exposure.
These expression patterns suggest roles in late embryonic development, chemosensory function, reproductive physiology, and vector-parasite interactions.
