Summary

Expression overview in Anopheles gambiae:
  • Highest in chemosensory appendages (antenna > palp) and head/carcass; strongly male-biased; very low in ovaries and hemolymph.
  • Digestive tract: enriched in anterior midgut of sugar-fed adults and hindgut of larvae; markedly depleted in larval salivary glands.
  • Blood feeding: whole-body levels drop after meals, while antennae transiently rise (12–24 h); HMBPP causes early decreases and a strong 24 h rebound.
  • Infection/mating: Plasmodium challenge elevates midgut levels and shifts RNA to polysomes; expression also increases late post‑oviposition and peaks late embryogenesis.
  • Together, patterns suggest a chemosensory/neuronal role modulated by feeding and infection, with additional functions in gut physiology across life stages.
