Summary

This gene exhibits extreme female-biased expression across all adult tissues and is dramatically upregulated (100-1000 fold) following blood feeding, peaking at 24 hours post-ingestion before returning to baseline by 48 hours.
  • Expression is highest in fat body and midgut tissues, reaching the 99th percentile during blood meal digestion
  • The gene is strongly downregulated (~28-fold) by 20-hydroxyecdysone (20E), a mating-associated hormone
  • Upregulation occurs following mating in female reproductive tissues
  • Expression is reduced during Plasmodium infection of the midgut
The consistent pattern of female-specific, blood meal-responsive expression in fat body tissue, combined with 20E regulation and post-mating induction, strongly suggests this gene encodes a vitellogenin or yolk protein involved in egg development and reproduction in Anopheles gambiae.
