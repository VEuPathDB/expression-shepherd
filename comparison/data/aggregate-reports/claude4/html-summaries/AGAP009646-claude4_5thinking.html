Summary

This gene exhibits highly dynamic temporal and spatial expression in Anopheles gambiae. The most striking pattern is dramatic upregulation at 4-5 hours post-egg laying (>500 TPM, 98th percentile), representing a >100-fold increase from baseline, suggesting critical involvement in early embryonic processes such as maternal-to-zygotic transition. In adults, expression is strongly midgut-specific, particularly enriched in the posterior midgut region (60-357 fold higher than other tissues), and shows substantial upregulation during malaria parasite infection (10-15 fold increase in infected midguts). The gene is also moderately expressed in testes and is downregulated following blood feeding (2-14 fold decrease). This expression profile suggests dual roles in early development and adult midgut function, potentially in digestion, metabolism, or host-parasite interactions.
