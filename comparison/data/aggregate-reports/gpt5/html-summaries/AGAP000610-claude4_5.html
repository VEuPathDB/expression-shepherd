Extreme Female-Specific Expression with Salivary Gland Enrichment in Anopheles gambiae

Model: Claude Sonnet 4.5

Summary

This gene exhibits dramatic female-biased expression across multiple experiments, with expression levels 64-128 fold higher in females than males.
  • Expression is particularly enriched in female salivary glands (900+ TPM, 99th percentile), suggesting specialized roles in blood-feeding or pathogen transmission
  • Within females, expression is highest in somatic carcass tissues rather than reproductive organs, indicating non-ovarian female-specific functions
  • Expression is adult-specific, absent in embryos and larvae, and shows suppression during early blood meal digestion
  • The gene responds strongly to 20-hydroxyecdysone in the atrium, implicating hormonal regulation of reproductive physiology
This expression pattern is characteristic of female-specific secretory proteins potentially involved in hematophagy, anti-hemostatic processes, or vector-pathogen interactions critical for malaria transmission.
