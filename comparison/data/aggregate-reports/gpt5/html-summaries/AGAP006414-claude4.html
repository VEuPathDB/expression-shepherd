Summary

This gene exhibits extremely high tissue-specific expression in the midgut of Anopheles gambiae, ranking consistently in the 96th-99th percentiles across multiple experiments. Expression shows strong female bias, with 4-40 fold higher levels in females compared to males or larvae. The gene displays
  • Circadian rhythmicity under light-dark conditions
  • Downregulation following blood feeding and malaria infection
  • Upregulation in insecticide-resistant populations
  • High expression in reproductive tissues including spermatheca after mating
. The consistent midgut enrichment and blood meal responsiveness suggest this gene likely plays important roles in digestive processes, blood meal processing, or metabolic functions critical for mosquito reproduction and survival.
