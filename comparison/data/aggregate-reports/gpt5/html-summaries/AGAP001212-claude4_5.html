Highly Expressed Midgut and Salivary Gland Protein with Adult-Specific, Female-Biased, and Age-Dependent Upregulation

Summary

This gene exhibits extremely strong tissue-specific expression in adult Anopheles gambiae, with highest levels in midgut and salivary glands—tissues critical for blood-feeding and pathogen transmission.
  • Expression is dramatically upregulated during the transition from pupal to adult stages and increases progressively with mosquito age (particularly in females)
  • The gene shows moderate female-biased expression and enrichment in metabolically active tissues (gastric caeca, fat body, hemocytes)
  • A robust circadian rhythm is observed in heads, with peak expression at dusk coinciding with host-seeking behavior
  • Expression responds dynamically to blood-feeding, with a biphasic pattern suggesting roles in both digestion and post-feeding recovery
The consistently high expression in vector-relevant tissues, combined with developmental and physiological regulation patterns, suggests this gene may encode a secretory or digestive protein important for mosquito feeding biology and potentially malaria transmission.
