Summary

This gene exhibits extreme female-specific expression in Anopheles gambiae, with approximately 100-fold higher expression in females compared to males. Expression is remarkably concentrated in adult female salivary glands, showing 1,500-2,700-fold higher levels than midgut tissues and ranking in the 99th percentile. The gene demonstrates adult-specific activation with essentially no expression in larvae or early developmental stages. Notably, expression is dramatically downregulated within 3 hours of blood feeding, dropping from the 89th to 26th percentile before gradually recovering by 96 hours. Additional regulatory complexity includes upregulation by 20-hydroxyecdysone in atrial tissue and apparent circadian rhythmicity under light-dark conditions. The extreme female and salivary gland specificity, combined with blood meal suppression, suggests this gene encodes a female-specific salivary component potentially involved in host-seeking or pre-feeding behaviors.
