Salivary and midgut enrichment with feeding, age, circadian, and mating regulation in Anopheles gambiae

Summary

Summary
  • In Anopheles gambiae, expression is extremely high in salivary glands and enriched in proventriculus/anterior midgut, lower in posterior midgut; hemolymph and carcass are high, while ovaries and male accessory glands are low.
  • Chemosensory tissues show bias: female maxillary palps > antennae; head/thorax small‑RNA signal is strong.
  • Expression increases robustly with adult age and shows strong circadian cycling in heads under constant darkness.
  • Feeding and drugs modulate levels: early 3 h suppression after a blood meal with later recovery/elevation; HMBPP induces at 1, 3, and 24 h with a 6 h dip; ivermectin partly offsets blood‑meal suppression; antenna peaks at ~36 h post‑feed.
  • Reproductive context: post‑mating induction in female tract and testes; mated female midguts > virgins.
  • Immune/stress: hemocytes up in infectious Plasmodium; Wolbachia effects differ by cell line; desiccation suppresses.
Speculation
  • The salivary/gut enrichment and feeding responsiveness suggest a secreted protein acting at the oral–gut interface (e.g., digestion, peritrophic matrix, or host interaction).
