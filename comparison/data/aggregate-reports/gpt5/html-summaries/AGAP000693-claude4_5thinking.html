Summary

This gene exhibits remarkable tissue-specific expression patterns in Anopheles gambiae.
  • It shows exceptional enrichment in digestive tissues, particularly the proventriculus (99.96th percentile), gastric caeca (97.56th percentile), and salivary glands (99.7th percentile)
  • Expression is dramatically upregulated upon mating in both male testes (~30-fold) and female reproductive tract
  • It displays strong developmental regulation, being nearly absent in early embryos but highly expressed in adults, with age-dependent increases throughout adult life
  • The gene is enriched in head/nervous tissues and shows circadian rhythmicity
  • Expression responds to environmental stresses including desiccation (downregulated) and Wolbachia infection (dramatically suppressed)
These patterns suggest specialized roles in digestive physiology, salivary gland function potentially related to blood-feeding or pathogen transmission, and post-mating reproductive processes.
