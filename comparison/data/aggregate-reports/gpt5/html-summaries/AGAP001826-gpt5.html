Salivary gland–biased, bloodmeal‑responsive expression across Anopheles gambiae development

Summary

Expression overview in Anopheles gambiae:
  • Strong tissue bias: very high in salivary glands and maxillary palps; elevated in head/hemolymph/fat body; low in midgut and ovary; palps greatly exceed antennae.
  • Development: rises from low early embryo to very high mid–late embryo; high in larvae and adults with a pupal dip; increases with adult age; larval gastric caeca are enriched.
  • Physiology: induced 3–24 h after blood feeding (transiently reduced at 48 h), with antennal induction at 12–48 h, and further boosted by ivermectin.
  • Infection: midgut invasion triggers a sharp rise; with Plasmodium falciparum, mRNA shifts to polysomes without total change.
  • Environment: higher under constant dark and modestly up with desiccation.
  • Together, patterns suggest roles in feeding‑associated secretion/chemosensation and stress‑responsive physiology.
