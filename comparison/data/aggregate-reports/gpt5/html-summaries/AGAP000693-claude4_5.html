Summary

This gene exhibits exceptional tissue-specific expression patterns across multiple physiological contexts in Anopheles gambiae.
  • Expression is dramatically enriched in salivary glands (24-fold over midgut, 99.7th percentile) and shows extreme regional specialization within the digestive tract, with highest levels in the proventriculus (35,270 TPM, 99.96th percentile)
  • The gene is strongly upregulated in response to mating in both male testes (28-fold increase) and female reproductive tissues (progressive increase to 24 hours post-mating)
  • Expression shows pronounced female-biased patterns and increases substantially with mosquito age
  • The gene responds dynamically to Wolbachia infection (54-fold downregulation) and displays circadian rhythmicity in head tissues
The tissue-specific enrichment in salivary glands and digestive organs, combined with mating-responsive regulation, suggests critical roles in blood-feeding physiology, digestion, and reproductive processes.
