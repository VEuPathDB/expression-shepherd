Summary

This gene exhibits highly tissue-specific expression in Anopheles gambiae, with strongest activity in the midgut where it shows dramatic upregulation during Plasmodium infection (10-fold increase). Expression is characterized by
  • A striking developmental peak during early embryogenesis (4-5 hours post-oviposition)
  • Strong midgut enrichment across multiple experimental contexts
  • Regional specialization within the digestive tract, favoring posterior regions
  • Downregulation following blood feeding
The gene demonstrates minimal expression in chemosensory tissues and shows modest sex-biased patterns. Given its consistent midgut-specific expression and strong response to parasite infection, this gene likely functions in digestive processes or host-pathogen interactions, potentially playing a role in the mosquito's response to malaria parasites.
