Midgut-Specific Digestive Gene Strongly Induced by Blood Feeding in Female Anopheles gambiae

Summary

This gene exhibits dramatic midgut-specific expression that is strongly induced by blood feeding in female Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes.
  • Expression peaks at 24 hours post-blood meal (99.32 percentile) and is highly enriched in midgut tissue (99.92 percentile), suggesting a critical role in blood digestion
  • The gene shows female-biased expression with 3-fold higher midgut levels in females versus males, consistent with female blood-feeding behavior
  • Circadian rhythmicity is observed in body tissues with 2-fold oscillations peaking during subjective night phases
  • Expression is also elevated in virgin females compared to larvae and gravid females, and shows transient upregulation at 6 hours post-mating
  • Modest upregulation occurs in insecticide-resistant mosquitoes (bendiocarb and deltamethrin)
The combined blood meal induction, midgut specificity, and temporal regulation strongly indicate this gene encodes a digestive enzyme or blood meal processing factor.
