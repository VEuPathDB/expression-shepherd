Summary

This gene exhibits exceptionally high expression in chemosensory organs (antenna and maxillary palps, 93-99th percentile) across both sexes in Anopheles gambiae, suggesting a critical role in olfaction and host-seeking behavior.
  • Expression is consistently male-biased across somatic tissues, particularly in head and carcass, while reproductive tissues show minimal expression
  • Blood feeding triggers rapid and sustained downregulation (approximately 4-fold decrease within 3 hours), with highest baseline expression in fat body tissue
  • The gene shows developmental regulation with larval peaks during L2-3 instar stages and transient upregulation at 46h embryogenesis near hatching time
  • Upregulation occurs in insecticide-resistant populations and following mating in females
The expression pattern suggests this gene encodes a chemosensory or odorant-binding protein involved in host detection and feeding behavior.
